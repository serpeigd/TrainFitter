# Nutrition — Calories, Macros, and Flexible Dieting

## Caloric needs (by goal)
- **Gaining muscle:** slight surplus.
- **Losing fat:** moderate and sustainable deficit (never aggressive). The evidence
  puts the sustainable rate at **0.5-1% of body weight per week**: below that,
  progress is needlessly slow; above it, the risk of losing muscle along with fat
  goes up.
- **Maintenance:** match the estimated expenditure.
- Cross-cutting priority: **sustainable** beats "optimal on paper."

## Protein (priority #1)
| Goal | Protein (g/kg/day) |
|---|---|
| Maintenance / general health (active person) | 1.4 – 2.0 |
| Gaining muscle (bulking) | 1.6 – 2.2 |
| Losing fat (preserving muscle) | 1.6 – 2.2 |

The Morton et al. meta-analysis (2018, with Schoenfeld as a co-author) finds that the
dose-response relationship with muscle gain **plateaus around 1.6 g/kg/day**; the
confidence interval extends to ~2.2 g/kg/day as a reasonable ceiling for trained
populations. The ISSN's 2017 position stand considers **1.4-2.0 g/kg/day sufficient
for most people who train**, with more recent and limited evidence suggesting
>3.0 g/kg/day might offer a small additional benefit to body composition in very
well-trained populations (not the priority for most clients).

> Pregnancy/breastfeeding increase needs → **refer to a professional for review**.

**Protein types:**
- *Fast* (whey concentrate 70-80% / isolate ~90%; meat, eggs, dairy): ideal
  post-workout.
- *Slow* (casein before bed; pea protein for vegans/intolerances): satiety and
  sustained release.

## Fats and carbohydrates
Adjusted based on remaining calories and the person's **preferences**. Real food as
the base; no "magic" macros.

## Flexible dieting (no bans)
- No food is forbidden: it's about **amounts and context**.
- Built around what the person actually likes and fits into their life (culture,
  schedule, budget). Teaches how to **fit treats in** without derailing the goal.

## Longevity focus (4 blocks, scientific basis)
1. **Vitamins, minerals, omega-3s, and thiol compounds:** vit. D, C, E, selenium,
   zinc, magnesium, EPA/DHA, garlic/cruciferous vegetables (glutathione precursors).
2. **Polyphenols / plant hormesis:** resveratrol, catechins, curcumin.
3. **Fermented foods:** yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, miso (microbiome).
4. **Fiber and prebiotics:** legumes, oats, inulin (butyrate, satiety, sleep).

## Fiber: reference amount
Official dietary guidelines (USDA/HHS) recommend **22-28 g/day for women and
28-34 g/day for men** (rule of thumb: ~14 g per 1,000 kcal consumed). Most of the
population falls well short — this is a realistic improvement target for nearly any
client intake, not just a longevity "extra."

## Myths rejected (with an explanation, never mockery)
Detox/cleanses · spot reduction · miracle/"fat-burning" diets · "muscle confusion" ·
"magic" fasting windows/schedules. What actually matters is total **caloric balance**
and adherence.
